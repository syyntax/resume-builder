<task name="import">
Transcribe this résumé into the schema.

1. Text: keep the original wording of every bullet, summary, title, and name. Only repair extraction damage: join words hyphenated across a line break, remove stray line breaks and doubled spaces, and drop bullet symbols (•, ▪, -, *) from the start of bullets.
2. Contact details go in `basics`: the person's name, their headline or professional title as `label`, email, phone, personal website as `url`, location, and profiles such as LinkedIn or GitHub (`network`, `username`, `url`). Write URLs as they appear; add `https://` only when the document clearly shows a web address without it.
3. Put a headline or title line under the name in `headline` as well as `basics.label`. Put the professional summary or profile paragraph in `summary`, word for word.
4. Experience: one `work` entry per role. When one company lists several titles (a promotion), make a separate entry for each title, in the document's order, each with its own dates. Split bullets that ran together into separate `highlights`. A role's introductory sentence that isn't a bullet goes in `summary`.
5. Dates: write them as YYYY-MM when the month is given, YYYY when only the year is given, and YYYY-MM-DD only when a full date is given. For a current role ("Present", "Current", "Now", or no end date on the most recent role), set `current` to true and leave `endDate` empty. Never guess a missing month or year; leave it out and add a note.
6. Skills: put only skills the document lists explicitly (in a skills section, sidebar, or similar list) in `skills`, keeping the document's groupings as `category` (for example "Languages", "Cloud & Infrastructure"). If skills aren't grouped, choose short, sensible categories. Technologies and tools that appear only inside bullets or descriptions go in `inferredSkills`, each with the short phrase that mentions it as `evidence`, so the person can confirm them. Don't list a skill in both.
7. Education, certifications, projects, awards, publications, volunteering, languages, and interests go in their own sections. Anything else, such as a security clearance, patents, speaking, or professional memberships, goes in `customSections` with the document's section title.
8. Tags: suggest two to four short lowercase topic tags for each work and project entry, such as "payments", "leadership", or "data-platform". Tags are metadata for matching, so inferring them is fine.
9. Import notes: add one for every field you're unsure about, with `path` pointing at it, such as `work[2].endDate`, `work[0].highlights[3]`, `education[1]`, or `basics.phone`. Use severity "warning" for anything the person should check (unreadable or ambiguous text, a date you couldn't determine, a possible duplicate role, content you couldn't place) and "info" for harmless observations (only a year was given, a section had no title). Keep each message short and specific, for example "End date missing; marked as current" or "Possible duplicate of work[1]".
